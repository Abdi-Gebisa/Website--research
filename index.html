What I am working on

These are not presented as finished discoveries. They are projects at different stages of learning, development, simulation, and experimental planning.

Time-Reversal Violation in Radiative Nuclear Beta Decay

DEVELOPING

My current nuclear-physics research idea explores a T-odd momentum correlation involving the beta particle, photon, and recoil system. A major part of the work is understanding final-state interactions, detector effects, and other systematic effects before interpreting a possible asymmetry.

Read the proposal →
Acknowledgment: I am grateful to Dr. John A Behr Senior Scientist at TRIUMF for guidance, discussion, or feedback that has helped me develop this project.

Experimental Investigation of Quantum Polarization and Malus' Law

PLANNING

This project is my practical entry into experimental quantum mechanics. I plan to investigate polarization and Malus' law quantitatively, record data, analyze uncertainty, and use the experience as a foundation for more advanced low-light and quantum-information experiments.

Read the project →
Acknowledgment: I am grateful to My Friends for support, advice, or scientific feedback related to this project.

Relativistic Time Dilation in Low-Momentum Muon Decay

TEAM PROJECT

I served as the theoretical physics lead for our school team in CERN's Beamline for Schools competition. Our proposal studied the precision boundary of relativistic time-dilation measurements in low-momentum muon decay.

Read the proposal →

04 · About me

A student building a research path one project at a time.

I study at Ambo University Special Secondary School in Ethiopia. My interests have grown from physics questions that I encountered at school and through independent study into a wider interest in nuclear physics, quantum mechanics, computation, and quantum information.

I do not want this website to look like a collection of claims. I want it to show the actual process: what I am asking, what I have learned, what I have tried, what I still do not understand, and where I want to go next.

“I am still learning, but I want to learn by doing real scientific work.”
